# Event-Driven Neuromorphic Computing on High-Performance Clusters

Traditional Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) process continuous real-valued activations across entire dense matrices at every single layer, incurring significant energy overhead during forward and backward passes.

In contrast, biological brains transmit information through discrete, sparse electrical pulses known as **spikes**. At the **TU/e Supercomputing Center**, our work on **Spike AI** centers around harnessing event-driven computation: neurons only perform mathematical operations when an action potential threshold is crossed.

## Why Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs)?

1. **Massive Energy Efficiency**: Up to 10×–100× lower power consumption on specialized neuromorphic silicon compared to conventional GPU architectures.
2. **Temporal Dynamism**: Natural alignment with streaming time-series sensor data and event-based silicon retinas (Dynamic Vision Sensors).
3. **Biological Realism**: Modeling non-linear synaptic plasticity and membrane potential decay dynamics.

## HPC Simulation Pipeline

Simulating millions of biologically plausible neurons on high-performance supercomputers requires distributed message-passing interfaces (MPI) and custom PyTorch CUDA kernels to efficiently schedule sparse spike trains without serialization bottlenecks.
